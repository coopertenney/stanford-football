Player Valuation

Enter a recruit profile to see the year-by-year distribution of what comparable players actually became, and what that is worth against a proposed offer.

Dollar figures carry roughly ±30% error. Outcome probabilities are fitted to 11 recruiting classes and calibrated against held-out data. The prices they are multiplied by come from an ESPN survey of 12+ GMs and 12+ agents (Aug 2026) at the Power 4 average — a practitioner survey, not an audited figure — scaled by the spend level you set below. No audited per-player compensation data exists publicly. Every figure is also shown in starter-seasons (SS), which carries no price assumption: 1.00 SS means "worth one season from a starter at this position, whatever you pay one." Special teams is excluded — the data cannot support a label.
Deal

Preference probability answers one question: you are indifferent between $0 for certain and a deal that wins the offer amount or loses it — what win probability makes you indifferent? 0.5 means risk neutral, which is what the predecessor assumed. Above 0.5 is risk averse.

Value assumptions — these drive every dollar figure

Spend level is your budget as a share of the Power 4 average and scales every dollar. Reference: 1.00 Power 4 average · ~0.6 ACC/Big 12 · ~0.25 a reduced conference distribution · ~0.15 Group of 6. Not inferred — set your own.

Tier multipliers are each outcome's worth relative to one starter-season. Sources disagree most on Depth: an ESPN within-position read implies 0.58–0.65, Opendorse starter-vs-backup bands imply 0.29–0.31, the PDR said 0.25. Shipping 0.55. Impact sits at 2.0 against reported elite pricing; true outliers reach 8–10× but that is a tail, not a tier. Bust is negative because a miss costs the roster spot and the money's alternative use — no market source prices it, so that one is reasoning.

Within-tier spread scales how much value varies inside a tier (1.0 = default). At 0 every starter is worth exactly the same and the p10–p90 range collapses to tier-mix alone.
